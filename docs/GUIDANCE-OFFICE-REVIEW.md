# Mind Bridge: clinical changes for guidance office review

Written for: guidance counselors who will approve or change how check-ins are scored. No technical background needed.
Full detail is in [SCORING.md](SCORING.md).

## What changed

1. **Staff see two extra screens on the quick check-in.** The seven questions already come from the PHQ-9 and GAD-7.
   Staff now also see the two-question depression screen (PHQ-2) and anxiety screen (GAD-2), each out of 6. A score of 3
   or more on either is the published "positive" mark.
2. **A positive screen raises a "low" result to "medium".** It never raises to "high" and never lowers anything.
3. **Students can choose a full check-in.** 16 standard questions (full PHQ-9, then full GAD-7), scored out of 48.
   Staff see each scale's score and its published band (minimal, mild, moderate, moderately severe, severe).
4. **A separate wellbeing card (WHO-5).** Five positive statements, scored 0 to 100. Below 50 is the published prompt to
   look further. It is only shown to the student (gently, with an offer to talk to a counselor) and to staff as extra
   information. **It never changes a risk level and never sends an alert.**

## Risk levels now

| Level | Quick check-in (7 questions, out of 21) | Full check-in (16 questions, out of 48) |
|---|---|---|
| High | Self-harm question above 0, or total 13 or more | Self-harm question above 0, or depression score 15+, or anxiety score 15+, or total 29+ |
| Medium | Total 7 to 12, or a positive PHQ-2 or GAD-2 | Depression or anxiety score 10+, or a positive PHQ-2 or GAD-2, or total 15+ |
| Low | Anything else | Anything else |

"High" is what triggers the urgent alert email to guidance staff.

## Known limits (please read)

* The quick check-in uses **adapted wording** and only **five of the nine** PHQ-9 questions and two of the seven GAD-7
  questions. It is a screening aid, **not** the validated PHQ-9 or GAD-7, so it shows no severity band. Only the optional
  full check-in does.
* Our percentage cutoffs (30% medium, 60% high) are our own, not published clinical cutoffs. The published two-question
  cutoff can be positive while our total says "low". That gap is why change 2 exists.
* None of this is a diagnosis. The app tells students so.

## Decisions we need from the guidance office

1. Is raising a positive PHQ-2/GAD-2 from "low" to "medium" acceptable, or should it go higher or stay informational?
2. Should a full check-in with a depression or anxiety score of 10 to 14 ("moderate") be "medium" (current) or "high"?
3. Is the WHO-5 card appropriate for students, and is the "below 50" message wording acceptable?
4. Should the adapted question wording be replaced with the exact published wording?
